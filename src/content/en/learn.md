## Learn about donating

This page is a **draft** prepared for the UDVASITO pilot. It restates the same general guidance as the app's FAQ and has not yet had its medical review. The blood bank at the hospital makes every final decision on the day.

## Who can donate

- You must be **18 or older** to use UDVASITO, and donors are generally 18 to 65 years old.
- Donors should weigh **at least 50 kg** and feel well on the day.
- After donating whole blood, the general wait is **90 days for men** and **120 days for women and everyone else**. The app works out your next eligible date automatically.
- Platelets are the part of blood that helps it clot. Platelet donors opt in separately, and platelet requests only go to donors who opted in.
- If you take regular medicine, have a long-term condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or recently had a tattoo or piercing, the honest answer is: the blood bank decides on the day. You can still register — the app will only alert you when you may be eligible.

## How donating through UDVASITO works

- When a patient near you needs your blood group, you get an alert. You decide every time — saying no or ignoring an alert is always fine.
- Your phone number is **never public**. It is shown to a family only after you tap "I can help" and confirm, and every time it is shown is recorded.
- At the hospital, the blood bank checks your details and screens you — including things the app never checks, like haemoglobin and blood pressure — and only then do you donate, if they accept you. Bring photo ID if you have it, and eat and drink normally beforehand unless the hospital says otherwise.

## Myths and facts

- **"Donors get paid."** Never. Donating through UDVASITO is always free and voluntary. If anyone asks for money to arrange a donor, report it in the app.
- **"The app decides if I am healthy enough."** No. The app only checks basic rules like age and time since your last donation. The blood bank makes the medical decision.
- **"I don't know my blood group, so I can't help."** You can register with "Not sure" — a blood bank can tell you your group, and replacement donation drives often test on the spot.
